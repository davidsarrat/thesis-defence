# Measured Kilimanjaro flight terrain

These assets are original 3D meshes with geographic surface UVs. They are not camera-projected scenery. The camera can move around Kibo, Mawenzi, Shira, their slopes and the surrounding plains while the measured relief and baked surface light remain attached to the ground.

## Measurements and licences

- NASA/NGA/USGS SRTM one-arcsecond elevation is public-domain U.S. government data, distributed through Mapzen/Tilezen Terrain Tiles on AWS Open Data. The source HGT tiles are S03–S05, E036–E038. See the [USGS product description](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/usgs-eros-archive-digital-elevation-shuttle-radar-topography-mission-srtm-1/) and [distribution attribution](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md#srtm).
- The regional colour uses Copernicus Sentinel-2 L2A imagery distributed by Element 84 Earth Search through AWS Open Data. **Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2024–2025.** See the [dataset](https://registry.opendata.aws/sentinel-2-l2a-cogs/) and [Copernicus Sentinel Data Legal Notice](https://sentinels.copernicus.eu/documents/247904/690755/Sentinel_Data_Legal_Notice).
- NASA Blue Marble Next Generation, public-domain NASA imagery, supplies the distant ground. Its existing attribution is in [the flight family source record](../SOURCE.md).

The source selection and raw asset fingerprints are retained in `metadata.json`. The primary clear summit acquisition is `S2B_37MCS_20250830_1_L2A`; a clearer September 2024 acquisition supplies the lower forest belt. Clouds, cirrus and cloud-shadow classes were excluded using each acquisition's Scene Classification Layer. The western foreground also uses narrow windows from the already-credited September 29 and September 6, 2025 acquisitions on tile 37MBS, to replace residual cloud-filled source gaps along the later flight path. August 30 was checked but supplied no additional clear pixels. These windows have the same Copernicus terms above; **Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2025**.

Summit ice uses the same dated footprint and unclipped 10 m Sentinel-2 B04/B03/B02 reflectance as the preceding measured mountain material. Its reflectance response is `0.65*r/(1+0.35*r)`. A 30 m colour prefilter within a 45 m boundary band softens source-pixel steps without changing the original alpha mask or its 50% contour. Restrained terrain-following relief and 16 m surface-normal variation allow the low sun to shade the fields. These are material treatments within the source resolution, not a new glacier survey or a synthetic snow cap.

Near-black source-shadow outliers on the western summit shoulder are locally softened in the delivered light map: 626 dark texels receive normalized neighbouring rock/ice radiance at 90% intensity, with a two-texel collar and 1.5-texel feather. This restrained radiance repair removes the hole-like appearance without changing the measured surface, crater, glacier footprint or surrounding baked light. The exact geographic bounds, thresholds and original fingerprints are retained in `metadata.json` and `refine-flight-terrain.py`.

## Geometry and light

The origin is 37.355° E, 3.067° S. Coordinates are metres in a local east/up/south frame: `x=east`, `y=elevation−Earth curvature`, `z=−north`. Elevations have no vertical exaggeration. The Earth radius is 6,371,000 m. SRTM supplies the mountain, regional slopes and distant relief; outside the measured regional extent, a coarse continuation blends into a 1,100 m analytic ground surface beyond the visible measured slopes. The continuation reaches a physical curved horizon.

The adaptive mesh retains approximately 30 m postings around Kibo, 60 m around Mawenzi and 90 m around Shira. The northern camera arc retains approximately 167 m postings from 16 to 34 km radius and −17° to 57° west of north, with an 83 m patch near the arrival. The sampling allocation replaces the original narrow corridor and keeps the total terrain near 390,000 triangles. Terrain outside these areas is progressively coarser. Adjacent patches share measured boundary samples. `height-query.f32.gz` supplies a bilinear terrain-height query for placing the 3D cloud belt; it is a separate, coarser grid and does not control the visible geometry.

`bake-flight-terrain.py` performs a Cycles diffuse bake of direct light, indirect light and material colour onto each object's geographic UV map. Low-sun shadows, terrain self-occlusion, sky fill and summit alpenglow are baked into the ground surface. The sun direction and warm, luminous sky illumination come from `flight-terrain-lighting.py`. Its 17-node sky graph and eight illumination constants were extracted unchanged from the original bake source; a node-and-link comparison passes, and the regenerated ice material is pixel-identical. The older per-bake source hashes remain recorded separately from the current reproduction modules. No camera or perspective projection is involved in the bake. Camera-distance haze and slowly drifting, low-amplitude cloud shadows are applied at runtime, so those effects keep moving with the 3D scene. Warm skylight gently lifts the occluded forest, while the distance haze draws the slopes toward the peach sky. Over the continuous evening, the retained baked light receives a bounded tint of at most +2.5% red/−0.8% green/−3.5% blue, and the procedural cloud shadows lengthen by at most 14%; the original terrain-shadow bake is not recomputed.

The mountain map is 4096 × 4096, the regional map 1024 × 1024 and the far map 512 × 512. A separate 4096 × 2048 intrinsic map retains the regional source detail across the northern orbit, from −30 to 30 km east and 0 to 36 km north. It uses the same measured material and low-sun illumination on the refined arc geometry, with approximately 14.6 × 17.6 m destination texels against the approximately 12 m source mosaic. It blends into the regional map over an 800 m geographic boundary band and adds no geometry or draw call. Its alpha channel records source confidence: only clear 2024 Sentinel vegetation, bare-ground and water samples that still agree with the mosaic receive the sharper detail. The original 24-pixel cloud-class exclusion and an additional 8-pixel smooth confidence falloff prevent the old nearest-filled cloud gaps from becoming sharp polygons. Unsupported areas retain the original regional light map. Combined estimated RGBA storage with mipmaps is 134.67 MiB. The mountain keeps its full resolution, and the larger foreground map spends detail over the visible orbit; only the distant regional/far maps are Lanczos-reduced from the original delivered light maps. Their original bake fingerprints remain intact, and the separate `deliveryResampling` record identifies the source and delivery sizes, hashes and operation. The maps retain the AgX Medium High Contrast transform at exposure +0.35; the runtime samples their display-referred RGB without a second filmic transform. The cache retains the linear EXR bakes and 16-bit AgX PNG intermediates. Each EXR is reloaded as Linear Rec.709 before the AgX export. Delivered WebPs include deterministic sub-code-value dither before eight-bit quantization. Actual sample counts, encoded bytes, mesh counts and hashes are recorded in `metadata.json`, with separate original-bake and exporter fingerprints.

The western foreground refinement keeps that same map size and allocation. Within approximately 37.08–37.30° E, 3.00–2.80° S, three 2025 acquisitions were screened; the two September windows provide 90.3% genuinely clear source coverage. Scene classes 4/5/6, a six-source-pixel exclusion collar and a separate bright cloud-fringe test screen the newer windows. An eight-pixel confidence fade and forty-pixel geographic blend join them into the original material. Cycles re-bakes this surface with the unchanged geometry, illumination and colour transform. Only that supported western footprint replaces the delivered corridor radiance, and its alpha extends the available measured detail. Remaining unsupported gaps still use the regional map; no fabricated ground pattern is added.

## Reproduction

The raw sources remain outside git in `/tmp/plane-plate10` and `/tmp/plane-plate12/cache`. Python dependencies are NumPy, SciPy, Pillow, OpenEXR, rasterio and Blender `bpy==4.2.0`. Reuse the measured DEM, colour and ice-material caches produced by the existing preparation scripts.

```sh
python docs/world/plane-africa/flight-terrain-lighting.py --cache /tmp/plane-plate12/cache
python docs/world/plane-africa/bake-flight-terrain.py --prototype
flock /tmp/plane-africa-cycles.lock python docs/world/plane-africa/bake-flight-terrain.py --samples 32
flock /tmp/plane-africa-cycles.lock python docs/world/plane-africa/bake-flight-terrain.py --layer corridor --samples 32
# For an existing 2K regional / 1K far delivery, reduce without a lighting rebake:
python docs/world/plane-africa/bake-flight-terrain.py --compact-existing
# Restore clear western ground and soften the summit's acquisition-shadow outliers:
flock /tmp/plane-africa-cycles.lock python docs/world/plane-africa/refine-flight-terrain.py
python docs/world/plane-africa/refine-flight-terrain.py --summit-only
```

Geometry files are gzip-compressed binary buffers. Their `FT01` header stores vertex and index counts, followed by little-endian Float32 positions, Float32 geographic UVs and Uint32 triangle indices. `plane-africa-landscape.js` decompresses them at preparation time and creates three retained meshes. The parent scene owns their rigid world registration, camera path and disposal.
