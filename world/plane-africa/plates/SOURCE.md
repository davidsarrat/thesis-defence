# Kilimanjaro ending plates

These are original offline renders of measured terrain, with satellite colour. They are not photographs, AI-generated scenery or a new glacier survey. The clouds and the evening's light are original procedural scene work. No non-commercial or share-alike assets are used.

## Measurements and imagery

- **NASA/NGA/USGS SRTM one-arcsecond elevation**, public-domain U.S. government data. Distribution: Mapzen/Tilezen Terrain Tiles, AWS Open Data. Source HGT tiles S03–S05, E036–E038; exact URLs and SHA-256 hashes are recorded in `metadata.json`. [USGS product description](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/usgs-eros-archive-digital-elevation-shuttle-radar-topography-mission-srtm-1/) and [distribution attribution](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md#srtm).
- **Copernicus Sentinel-2 L2A true-colour imagery**, 2024 and 2025, supplied as open Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs through Element 84 Earth Search / the AWS Open Data registry. **Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2024–2025.** [Dataset and open access terms](https://registry.opendata.aws/sentinel-2-l2a-cogs/) and [Copernicus Sentinel Data Legal Notice](https://sentinels.copernicus.eu/documents/247904/690755/Sentinel_Data_Legal_Notice). The notice allows reproduction, distribution, adaptation and combination for lawful commercial and non-commercial use, with the attribution. This is the Sentinel data itself, not the EOX s2cloudless mosaic.
- **NASA Blue Marble Next Generation**, public-domain NASA imagery, already recorded in [the family source record](../SOURCE.md), supplies the coarse distant apron.

The fine crop is 36.90–37.80° E, 3.50–2.70° S. A 1501 × 1061 summit patch samples the original one-arcsecond relief at approximately 30 m; a 1201 × 1067 surrounding grid and a 501 × 501 distant grid retain the real crater rim, Kibo, Mawenzi, Shira and their saddle. Elevations remain unexaggerated. The fine material is an 8192 × 7282 geographic RGB mosaic. The first chosen acquisition is `S2B_37MCS_20250830_1_L2A`, whose summit is clear. Later choices fill only missing pixels after excluding cloud, cirrus and cloud-shadow classes with each product's Sentinel Scene Classification Layer. Summit snow and glacier patches come from the dated satellite colour; no uniform snow cap is painted. A clearer 14 September 2024 acquisition is preferred on the lower slopes, below 4350 m, and tapers into the unchanged 2025 summit material by 4700 m. Its SCL invalid classes are dilated by about 300 m before compositing. Residual off-swath gaps and neutral cloud fringes below 4000 m are filled from neighbouring measured RGB; this image cleanup is recorded in the metadata and does not change the DEM or summit ice. Exact scene identifiers and dates are in `metadata.json`.

`prepare-ending-ice.py` retrieves small windows of the original 10 m B04/B03/B02 reflectance bands from the same acquisition sequence. These recover variation lost when the display RGB clips bright ice to white. The valid clear measurements cover 91.87% of the existing Kibo footprint (89.82% across the broader mountain crop); unobserved pixels retain the original RGB. The metadata records the band windows, transforms, hashes and independent Collection 1 checks of the effective zero offset. The measured reflectance retains the acquisition's illumination and is not an intrinsic albedo map. Ice cliffs smaller than the 30 m DEM are not resolved geometry, and small fields occupy only a few pixels at slide framing.

## Render and delivery

`render-ending-plate.py` uses Blender Cycles on the CPU: measured terrain, physically shaded materials, low sunlight, sky illumination and procedural volume-scattering clouds. The sky's peach, soft pink and apricot radiance follows the deck's PRBB coast palette. A low golden sun with a broad halo grazes the measured gullies; warm neutral fill preserves the dark forest, moorland and volcanic rock. Aerial perspective starts beyond 15 km so orange scattering does not overwhelm dark forest reflectance. Terrain aerial perspective is a distance-based scattering approximation. Summit ice uses unclipped linear Sentinel-2 B04/B03/B02 reflectance inside the existing bright-pixel/altitude footprint. A separate float EXR retains measured variation that the eight-bit display RGB clips to white. The material multiplies this reflectance by 0.8 and adds restrained 16 m surface normals, roughness and terrain-following edge relief; no broad synthetic ice colour is painted. A 15 m linear-colour prefilter, limited to a 20 m boundary band, softens source-pixel steps below slide-pixel scale. The original mask and its 50% contour are unchanged. These shader details are artistic material treatment, not a new glacier geometry survey. The dated ice footprint and the measured DEM are unchanged. The distant apron uses real Earth curvature, rather than the earlier 620-unit miniature planet; only the farthest continuation is an analytic surface. The colour transform is Blender AgX. The sun and cloud forms are original render content, with no external HDRI or cloud photographs.

- `sky.webp`: the matched evening sky and hazy solar disc.
- `land.webp`: terrain and mountain, with straight alpha above the horizon.
- `cloud-far.webp`: a long, periodically lit field of broken slope-cloud fragments, with straight alpha.
- `cloud-near.webp`: a long field of nearer volumetric cloud banks, with straight alpha.
- `environment.webp`: the same sky as an equirectangular environment for the live aircraft.

The sky and terrain plates include an overscan margin: the central 88% of each dimension is the final 38° vertical field of view. The environment is rolled horizontally by 315 pixels to align its sun with the live aircraft’s Three.js coordinates. `metadata.json` records the photographic camera, visible crop, samples, sun direction and output dimensions. WebP RGB is already display-referred sRGB with AgX baked in; the runtime must not apply a second filmic transform. Alpha is straight, not premultiplied.

Sky and terrain are 3072 × 1728; the environment is 1024 × 512. The near cloud strip is 8192 × 768 and the far strip 4096 × 512. The cloud fields use orthographic cameras, separate from the photographic terrain set: this deliberate depth-layer compositing keeps their visible scale consistent while they stream. They use the same sky radiance, directional sun and display grade. Their volumes shade one another; the terrain is not a cloud occluder. Uniform world illumination and duplicated edge volumes make the horizontal field periodic. The near and far views consume only 22% and 44% of their strips, respectively, crossing one frame in 60 and 240 seconds. They contain more than three minutes of unique visible content before any repetition.

The top-down composition bands are 55–112% of the frame for the near clouds and 40–72% for the far fragments. The near layer extends below the frame to leave a calm pale surface behind the acknowledgement band. `metadata.json` records the precise strip projection, sampling settings, output dimensions and camera directions. Terrain uses 128 Cycles samples; sky and environment use 64, the far cloud strip 48 and the near strip 32, with adaptive threshold 0.015 and OpenImageDenoise. Cloud volumes use two scattering bounces and a step rate of 2. Standalone sky, environment and cloud passes omit terrain geometry. The `--draft` switch reduces only terrain geometry when producing an all-layer preview; it never changes cloud geometry or output resolution.

The AgX Medium High Contrast transform uses exposure +0.35, followed by a restrained warm print balance. Sky and environment retain a 16-bit PNG intermediate; print balance and deterministic sub-code-value dither are applied before eight-bit quantization, preventing contour bands. Both use lossless WebP so this dither survives. Terrain and clouds use eight-bit PNG intermediates and WebP quality 99 with lossless alpha. These settings are recorded per layer in the metadata.

The raw source data, 8K intermediary mosaic and full-resolution PNG renders stay outside git in `/tmp/plane-plate10`. Reproduce with Python 3.11, Blender's `bpy==4.2.0`, NumPy, SciPy, Pillow, rasterio, requests and OpenEXR:

```sh
python docs/world/plane-africa/prepare-ending-plate.py --cache /tmp/plane-plate10
python docs/world/plane-africa/prepare-ending-forest.py --cache /tmp/plane-plate10
python docs/world/plane-africa/prepare-ending-ice.py --cache /tmp/plane-plate10
python docs/world/plane-africa/render-ending-plate.py --cache /tmp/plane-plate10 --width 3072 --samples 64 --draft --layer sky
python docs/world/plane-africa/render-ending-plate.py --cache /tmp/plane-plate10 --width 3072 --samples 64 --draft --layer environment
python docs/world/plane-africa/render-ending-plate.py --cache /tmp/plane-plate10 --width 3072 --samples 128 --layer land
python docs/world/plane-africa/render-ending-plate.py --cache /tmp/plane-plate10 --width 3072 --samples 48 --draft --layer cloud-far
python docs/world/plane-africa/render-ending-plate.py --cache /tmp/plane-plate10 --width 3072 --samples 32 --draft --layer cloud-near
```
